AI Fact Checker for News, Claims, and Forwards

Use Kaval when a claim sounds important but the source is unclear. It checks whether a headline, forward, screenshot, or social post is backed by credible evidence before you repeat it.

what to look for.

  • Breaking news claims that may be old, miscaptioned, or unsupported
  • Political, health, disaster, finance, and public-warning forwards
  • Screenshots that omit source context or dates
  • Claims that mix true details with misleading conclusions
  • Advice that could cause financial, medical, or personal harm

useful context.

Evidence first

A useful fact-check does not rely on confident wording alone. Kaval looks for corroborating sources, original reporting, fact-checking references, and contradictions before summarising the evidence.

Designed for messy claims

Online claims arrive as copied text, cropped screenshots, viral headlines, video captions, and group messages. Kaval focuses on extracting the checkable claim before searching for evidence.

Clear uncertainty

Some claims cannot be verified immediately. In those cases, the answer should say what is known, what is missing, and whether the safest move is to wait before sharing.

questions people ask.

Is Kaval a replacement for professional fact-checkers?

No. Kaval is for triaging everyday claims quickly. For high-stakes questions, use it alongside original sources, official information, and professional reporting.

Can Kaval fact-check screenshots?

Yes. Send the screenshot with the claim attached to it. Kaval can check whether the content appears supported, outdated, edited, or missing context.

What happens when there is not enough evidence?

The safest answer is uncertainty. Kaval explains what is missing and recommends waiting before sharing the claim as true.
